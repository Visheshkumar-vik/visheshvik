Biomedical

A stent, from flat pattern to blood flow

This is the real stent from my capstone, StentForge. My Python tool draws the flat pattern and rolls it into a tube. Ansys Mechanical crimps the stent and expands it in a narrowed artery. Ansys Fluent then runs pulsatile blood flow past the struts.

Ansys SimAI learns from every run. LEAP Australia plans a blog post and a video on this SimAI work, and we are preparing a paper for publication. I am the lead author, in a team of three.

Thermal

A heat exchanger, from CAD to temperature

I built the shell-and-tube heat exchanger in SpaceClaim. I meshed it in ANSYS Meshing, with refined boundary layers. ANSYS Fluent then solved the flow and the heat transfer. I checked the outlet temperatures against analytical predictions.

  • SpaceClaim
  • ANSYS Meshing
  • ANSYS Fluent
  • CFD-Post

Process

A mixing elbow, from CAD to mixture

I built a pipe elbow with a swirler in SpaceClaim and meshed it in ANSYS Meshing. ANSYS Fluent solved the species transport of the two streams. The results show how the swirl improves the mixing.

The same method applies to chemical dosing and to mineral processing circuits.

  • SpaceClaim
  • ANSYS Meshing
  • Species transport
  • CFD-Post

About

Final-year B.Eng. (Mechanical) student at RMIT, specialising in aerospace engineering, mechatronics and autonomous systems. I write CFD pipelines, deploy nosecones and program drones.

I grew up in Delhi — fireflies and chai and a grandfather's warmth — and landed in Melbourne chasing something I couldn't quite name yet. I've since designed deployable nosecones for L2-class rockets, programmed autonomous drones, and built CFD pipelines for cardiovascular stents with LEAP & ANSYS.

I believe the most interesting people refuse to be one thing. I'm an engineer who reads Camus, a poet who understands fluid dynamics, and someone who thinks Feynman's greatest achievement was making physics feel like joy. I also write poems — usually at 2 a.m., when the equations can't hold what I'm thinking.

Currently building

Iradda — intention into action.

Co-founded with Ishika Chaudhary, a policy researcher who works on a NITI Aayog project at IIM Ahmedabad and has worked at NITI Aayog before. Iradda translates institutional knowledge about gender equality into mentorship, skills and access — the kind that reaches the women who actually need it.

The word Iradda draws from the Sanskrit Sankalpa: the resolve to act with purpose. The most useful policy insights shouldn't live only in policy circles.
